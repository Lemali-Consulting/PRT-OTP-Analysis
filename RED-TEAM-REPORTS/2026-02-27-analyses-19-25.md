## Out of Scope

1. **Analysis 20 — Reverse causality framing.** FINDINGS.md (line 17) interprets the negative contemporaneous correlation (median r = -0.18) as "reverse causality: months when fewer people ride see better OTP." This is a reasonable interpretation but is stated as fact rather than hypothesis. An alternative explanation (confounders like weather or holidays affecting both) is mentioned in Discussion but not in the Key Numbers section. This is a framing concern, not a checklist item.

2. **Analysis 21 — "Zero routes recovered" claim robustness.** FINDINGS.md states "zero of 93 routes have recovered to pre-COVID ridership levels." This depends on the choice of recovery period end date (Oct 2024). If ridership data were extended even a few months, some routes might cross the threshold. The claim is technically correct for the data period but is presented as a strong systemic conclusion. Not a checklist item but worth noting for interpretive caution.

3. **Analysis 23 — Garage as corridor proxy.** FINDINGS.md correctly reinterprets the garage effect as a corridor-congestion proxy (lines 47-53). However, the code does not include any direct test of this interpretation (e.g., adding a traffic variable or corridor indicator to the OLS model). The interpretation is plausible but unverified within this analysis. Analysis 27 is referenced but not available for cross-validation in this review.

4. **Analysis 24 — Sunday/Holiday conflation.** The `SUN.` day type in `ridership_monthly` likely includes holidays (based on the label "Sunday/Holiday" in the chart at line 157). This means the Sunday recovery rate (83.9%) may be inflated or deflated by holidays that fall on different day-of-week across years. Not a checklist item but worth noting for seasonal analyses.
